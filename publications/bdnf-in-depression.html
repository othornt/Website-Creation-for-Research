Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) in Depression: A Mini Review

A concise review of how BDNF signaling influences depressive disorders and how emerging interventions modulate neurotrophic pathways for therapeutic gain.

2023 · International Neuropsychiatric Disease Journal 20 (3), 47–56 DOI: 10.9734/INDJ/2023/v20i3399 Solo author

Abstract

Depression is a complex psychiatric disorder that significantly impacts millions of individuals worldwide. Emerging evidence implicates brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)—a critical regulator of neuronal plasticity—in both the pathophysiology and treatment of depression. This review examines the role of BDNF in the brain, its relationship to depression in preclinical and clinical studies, and its potential as a diagnostic or prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target. It summarizes how pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic interventions influence BDNF levels and outlines key challenges and future directions for leveraging BDNF in precision mental health care.

Key Findings

  • Reduced BDNF availability is associated with depressive phenotypes, while successful treatment often restores BDNF signaling.
  • Pharmacologic, neuromodulation, and behavioral interventions can elevate BDNF, offering biomarkers for response tracking.
  • Future precision-medicine approaches may rely on BDNF profiles to customize care pathways.
  • Challenges remain in standardizing BDNF measurement across clinical contexts.

Research Approach

Owen synthesized data from translational neuroscience literature, comparing molecular findings with patient outcomes. The review contextualizes BDNF within broader neuroplasticity frameworks and highlights gaps in biomarker-driven care.
