The Role of the Hippocampus in Borderline Personality Disorder

A deep dive into structural and functional hippocampal abnormalities associated with borderline personality disorder and their implications for emotion regulation and decision-making.

2023 · International Neuropsychiatric Disease Journal 19 (2), 28–38 DOI: 10.9734/INDJ/2023/v19i2370 Co-authors: Wenjun Li · Hunter Cole · Isabella Cólon

Abstract

The review summarizes converging neuroimaging evidence that individuals with BPD exhibit reduced hippocampal volume, altered activation patterns, and disrupted network connectivity. These neural differences contribute to emotional dysregulation, impulsivity, and unstable self-image, with trauma and chronic stress emerging as key mechanistic drivers.

Key Findings

  • Hippocampal volume reductions correlate with heightened emotional volatility and memory disturbances in BPD.
  • Functional studies reveal atypical hippocampal activation and connectivity with prefrontal and limbic regions.
  • Trauma exposure and chronic stress may mediate hippocampal alterations, offering targets for preventative care.
  • Future research should integrate computational models to link hippocampal changes with decision-making deficits.

Research Approach

The authors synthesized findings from MRI volumetrics, fMRI activation studies, and connectivity analyses, mapping hippocampal differences to clinical symptom clusters. The review highlights methodological gaps and recommends multimodal imaging strategies for upcoming investigations.
