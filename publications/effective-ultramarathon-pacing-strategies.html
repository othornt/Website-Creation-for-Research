Effective Ultramarathon Pacing Strategies on Road and Trail

A narrative review detailing how distance, terrain, environment, and athlete profile shape pacing tactics across the ultramarathon spectrum.

2023 · Asian Journal of Advanced Research and Reports 17 (4), 1–11 DOI: 10.9734/AJARR/2023/v17i4474 Solo author

Abstract

Ultramarathons have experienced remarkable growth since the inaugural Comrades Marathon in 1921, intensifying the need to understand how pacing shapes performance. This review synthesizes literature on pacing strategies across road, track, and mountain ultramarathons, showing how factors such as age, distance, performance tier, and competition context govern optimal approaches. It compares techniques used to counter speed loss, manage environmental stressors, coordinate pre-cooling, and adapt strategies between terrains. By highlighting similarities, distinctions, and gaps, the article equips athletes, coaches, and researchers to tailor pacing for diverse ultramarathon settings.

Key Findings

  • Negative or even splits dominate successful road ultramarathons, while variable pacing benefits technical trail courses.
  • Precooling, heat mitigation, and fueling plans must be synchronized with pacing to avoid precipitous slowdowns.
  • Experience, age, and performance tier influence whether athletes can sustain aggressive early paces without collapse.
  • Research gaps persist around individualized pacing algorithms that integrate terrain, weather, and biometric feedback.

Research Approach

Owen analyzed case studies, race telemetry, and physiological research to contrast pacing strategies across surfaces and elevation profiles. The review integrates practical coaching frameworks with empirical data to inform training plans for elites and newcomers alike.
