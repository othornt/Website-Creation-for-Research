The Psychological Indicators of Success in Ultrarunning

A research synthesis identifying the mental frameworks—self-efficacy, mental toughness, emotional intelligence, and mood regulation—that differentiate elite ultramarathon performers.

2023 · Research Square Preprint DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-2675495/v1 Co-authors: Sophia Ly · Isabella Colón · Hunter Cole · Wenjun Li

Abstract

Ultrarunning has grown over 345% in the last decade. During events that stretch from six hours to multiple days, athletes with varying backgrounds must manage intense emotions, fatigue, and endurance demands. Despite the sport's rapid growth, a comprehensive synthesis of the psychological advantages linked to ultrarunning success has been lacking. This review surveys literature identified across four databases between December 2022 and January 2023 and highlights how elevated self-efficacy, mental toughness, emotional intelligence, and reduced mood variability characterize successful ultramarathoners. It underscores the value of cognitive strategies that stabilize mood and calls for deeper investigation into the endurance-specific traits that underpin elite performance.

Key Findings

  • High self-efficacy, mental toughness, and emotional intelligence underpin sustainable performance in multiday events.
  • Consistent mood regulation—especially reduced negative mood variability—enables athletes to navigate fatigue and adversity.
  • Evidence gaps remain around longitudinal interventions that can train these attributes in emerging endurance athletes.
  • Future research should examine how psychological indicators interact with nutrition, pacing, and environmental stressors.

Research Approach

The team conducted a structured literature review across four scholarly databases, screening and synthesizing studies that linked psychological constructs to ultramarathon outcomes. Comparative analysis aligned mental skills with measured performance indicators and athlete narratives.
