Borderline Personality Disorder and Neuroplasticity: A Review

A comprehensive review of structural and functional plasticity alterations in borderline personality disorder (BPD) and the therapeutic interventions that may restore adaptive neural pathways.

2023 · International Neuropsychiatric Disease Journal 19 (1), 1–8 DOI: 10.9734/INDJ/2023/v19i2367 Co-authors: Wenjun Li · Hunter Cole · Isabella Cólon

Abstract

Emotional dysregulation, impulsivity, and unstable relationships characterize BPD. This review synthesizes evidence that neuroplasticity impairments in the prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and hippocampus underpin these symptoms and evaluates emerging treatments—including dialectical behavior therapy—that target plasticity to improve outcomes.

Key Findings

  • BPD is associated with diminished neuroplasticity across emotion-regulation circuits, affecting cognition and behavior.
  • Psychotherapeutic approaches such as DBT show promise in normalizing plasticity markers and reducing symptom burden.
  • Neuroplasticity-focused interventions require rigorous longitudinal evaluation to quantify sustained benefits.
  • Understanding plasticity may explain heterogeneous responses to pharmacological and psychotherapeutic care.

Research Approach

The authors evaluated neuroimaging, neuroendocrine, and behavioral studies describing plasticity in BPD, prioritizing convergent evidence from clinical trials and mechanistic investigations. Comparative synthesis linked neural targets with symptom domains to guide future translational studies.
