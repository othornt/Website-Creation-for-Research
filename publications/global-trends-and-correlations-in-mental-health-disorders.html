Global Trends and Correlations in Mental Health Disorders

A population-scale analysis covering 6,420 observations between 1990 and 2019 to map mental health burdens, workforce risks, and gender inequities across world regions.

2024 · International Neuropsychiatric Disease Journal 21 (2), 36-53 DOI: 10.9734/INDJ/2024/v21i2425 Co-author: Wenjun Li

Abstract

This original paper delivers a comprehensive analysis of the prevalence and trends of mental health disorders (n = 6,420) from 1990 to 2019. Using curated datasets and statistical methods, it explores increasing rates of mental health conditions with focused attention on healthcare workers, gender disparities, and geographic variation. The study identifies healthcare professionals as a particularly high-risk group, surfaces regional differences that call for tailored policy responses, and highlights gender-specific vulnerabilities. Findings guide targeted mental health programs for at-risk populations and outline priorities for future global surveillance research.

Key Findings

  • Sustained global increase in diagnosed mental health disorders across three decades of surveillance.
  • Healthcare workers recorded disproportionately high burden, underscoring the need for occupational safeguards.
  • Gender disparities persist worldwide, pointing to structural inequities in access to care and socio-cultural stressors.
  • Geographic variation suggests that regional policy responses and resource allocations must be tailored to local trends.

Research Approach

Owen led the statistical modeling and visualization of a curated dataset spanning 6,420 observations. Time-series comparisons and stratified analyses were used to evaluate prevalence changes, occupational exposure, and gendered outcomes across global regions.
